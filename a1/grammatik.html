Teil A

Grammatik-Basis (grammar basics)

How German sentences are built: word types, der/die/das, plural, verbs, word order, and how to ask and answer.

1 · Wortarten (word types)

Every sentence is built from a few kinds of words. Knowing the type tells you what rules apply: nouns need an article, verbs change their ending, adjectives describe.

Nomen noun

A naming word. Anything you can point at or put "the" in front of: a thing, a person, an animal, a place, or an idea.

Test: can you say "the …"? the book, the woman, the coffee → noun.

In German a noun always starts with a capital letter.

Buch (book), Frau (woman), Hamburg, Kaffee (coffee)

Artikel article

The little word in front of a noun, like "the" and "a" in English.

In German it also tells you the noun's gender: der (blue), die (red), das (green).

der, die, das (the) · ein, eine (a)

Verb verb

A doing word or a being word. It says what someone does (drink, live, work) or what someone is / has (be, have, be called).

Test: can you put "I" or "to" in front? I drink, to live → verb.

The ending changes with the person: ich wohne, du wohnst.

trinken (to drink), wohnen (to live), sein (to be), heißen (to be called)

Pronomen pronoun

A short word you use instead of a name or a noun, so you don't repeat it.

"Anna is nice. She is from Spain." → she = pronoun.

ich (I), du (you), er (he), sie (she / they), es (it), wir (we), ihr (you all), Sie (you, polite)

Adjektiv adjective

A describing word. It tells you what a noun is like: size, colour, age, how good.

Test: "The flat is …?" big, new, nice → adjective.

groß (big), neu (new), schön (nice), gut (good), rot (red)

Adverb adverb

An extra-information word. It answers when? where? how? how much? It never changes its form.

"I work today / here / a lot." → adverb.

heute (today), hier (here), sehr (very), auch (also), gern (gladly)

Präposition preposition

A small place / time / direction word that links a noun to the rest of the sentence.

"I live in Berlin. I come from Spain. I go with Anna."

in (in), aus (from), mit (with), bei (at), um (at, for clock time), am (on)

Konjunktion conjunction

A joining word. It glues two words or two sentences together.

"tea and coffee", "small but nice", "tea or coffee?"

und (and), oder (or), aber (but), denn (because)

2 · der, die, das (the)

Every German noun has a grammatical gender. English has one word "the"; German has three. The gender is often not logical (das Mädchen = the girl is neuter), so learn every noun together with its article, like one word: die Lampe, never just Lampe.

dermaskulin (masculine)
  • masculine people: der Mann, der Vater
  • days, months, seasons: der Montag
  • often ending -er (tools/jobs): der Computer, der Lehrer
diefeminin (feminine)
  • feminine people: die Frau, die Mutter
  • most nouns ending -e: die Lampe, die Tasche
  • -ung, -heit, -keit, -ion, -in: die Wohnung, die Freundin
dasneutral (neuter)
  • -chen, -lein: das Mädchen
  • many short words: das Buch, das Kind, das Haus
  • English loan words: das Handy, das Auto
Endings tell the genderLook at the end of the noun first. Outlined = always, the rest = usually

-er-lingdaysmonthsseasonsmasculine people
der Computer, der Montag, der Juli, der Sommer, der Mann

-e-ung-heit-keit-ion-schaft-in
die Lampe, die Wohnung, die Information, die Freundin

Trick: -chen makes things small and cute, and "small = neutral": das Mädchen (girl), das Brötchen (bread roll). A verb used as a noun is always das: das Essen, das Radfahren.

Reminder · Colour your notes. Write nouns in your notebook in colour: blue = der, red = die, green = das.

der/die/das = a specific thing (the). ein/eine = any one thing (a). Das ist eine Lampe. Die Lampe ist schön. (That is a lamp. The lamp is beautiful.)

Trick: "die" ends with e, so "eine" gets an e too. Same for kein → keine. der and das are twins: they share "ein".

Next step: Akkusativ (the object form)

When a noun is the thing you have, want or buy (after haben, möchten, brauchen, kaufen), only the der words change. You need this in Sprechen Teil 3.

Akkusativ: only der changesAfter haben, möchten, brauchen, kaufen

Trick: "the masculine needs an N": den, einen, keinen. Everybody else stays the same. Ich möchte einen Kaffee und eine Banane.

4 · Singular und Plural (one and many)

German has several plural endings. There is no single rule, so learn the plural with the noun: der Tisch, die Tische. Dictionaries write it short: Tisch, der, -e. The ¨ symbol means the vowel gets an umlaut: Buch, das, ¨-er → Bücher.

-e / ¨-emany der wordsder Tisch → die Tische · der Stuhl → die Stühle

-sloan words, ending in a voweldas Handy → die Handys · das Auto → die Autos
– / ¨-er, -el, -chender Computer → die Computer · der Apfel → die Äpfel

Modalverben (modal verbs: can, must, want, may, would like)

Five small verbs let you ask, request, refuse and explain: können, müssen, wollen, dürfen, möchten. You need them in the speaking test (Teil 3: requests), in the writing test and in almost every conversation. They all work the same way: the modal verb is in place 2, the second verb jumps to the very end.

The sentence sandwichmodal = left hand (place 2) · infinitive = right hand (the end)

Trick: everything else (time, place, objects) goes inside the sandwich. The second verb is always the infinitive (-en): Ich kann kochen, never "Ich kann koche".

7 · Fragen, Antworten, längere Sätze (questions, answers, longer sentences)

Asking and answering is half of the speaking exam. Build every question with the same three steps, then answer by swapping the person.

W-Frage in 3 Schritten (W-question in 3 steps)

  1. W-word: what do you want to know? Place → Wo. Origin → Woher. Name → Wie. Time → Wann. Thing → Was.
  2. Verb: conjugate it for the person you ask: du wohnst, Sie wohnen.
  3. Person + rest: du / Sie, then the rest of the sentence. End with ?

Reminder · Modal verbs have no ending in ich and er/sie/es. ich kann, er kann (NOT er kannt). ich muss, sie muss. The 2nd verb at the end is always the dictionary form (infinitive, -en): Er kann gut kochen.
Reminder · Is it separable? Small words like auf, an, ein, mit, fern, aus, zu at the front of a verb usually split off. The stress is on that small word: AUFstehen. In the dictionary it often appears as auf|stehen.

Trick 2 · Konnektoren: Position 0 (connectors: position zero)

und, aber, oder, denn (and sondern) join two sentences but take no place in the train. After them, a completely normal sentence starts.

Memory trick: ADUSO = Aber, Denn, Und, Sondern, Oder. They are the couplings between two trains, and a coupling has no seat, so these five are position 0. Every other linking word (dann, danach, zuerst, später, heute) takes place 1 and pushes the person behind the verb.
