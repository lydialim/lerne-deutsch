Teil A

Grammatik-Basis (grammar basics)

How German sentences are built: word types, der/die/das, plural, verbs, word order, and how to ask and answer.

1 · Wortarten (word types)

Every sentence is built from a few kinds of words. Knowing the type tells you what rules apply: nouns need an article, verbs change their ending, adjectives describe.

Nomen noun

A thing, person, place or idea. Always starts with a capital letter in German.

Buch, Frau, Hamburg, Kaffee

Artikel article

The little word before a noun. Shows the gender.

der, die, das · ein, eine

Verb verb

An action or state. The ending changes with the person (ich, du, er …).

heißen, wohnen, sein, trinken

Pronomen pronoun

Replaces a noun or a person.

ich, du, er, sie, es, wir, ihr, Sie

Adjektiv adjective

Describes a noun: how it is.

gut, neu, schön, klein

Adverb adverb

Says when, where, how much. Never changes its form.

heute, hier, sehr, auch

Präposition preposition

Shows place, origin, direction.

in, aus, mit, bei

Konjunktion conjunction

Joins words or sentences.

und, oder, aber

Beispiel (example), word by word

Reminder · Nouns have a capital letter. If a word in the middle of a sentence starts with a capital, it is a noun (or a name, or Sie = formal you). That is your first clue for der/die/das.

2 · der, die, das (the)

Every German noun has a grammatical gender. English has one word "the"; German has three. The gender is often not logical (das Mädchen = the girl is neuter), so learn every noun together with its article, like one word: die Lampe, never just Lampe.

dermaskulin (masculine)
  • male people: der Mann, der Vater
  • days, months, seasons: der Montag
  • often ending -er (tools/jobs): der Computer, der Lehrer
diefeminin (feminine)
  • female people: die Frau, die Mutter
  • most nouns ending -e: die Lampe, die Tasche
  • -ung, -heit, -keit, -ion, -in: die Wohnung, die Freundin
dasneutral (neuter)
  • -chen, -lein: das Mädchen
  • many short words: das Buch, das Kind, das Haus
  • English loan words: das Handy, das Auto
Endings tell the genderLook at the end of the noun first. Outlined = always, the rest = usually

-er-lingdaysmonthsseasonsmen

Trick: -chen makes things small and cute, and "small = neutral": das Mädchen (girl), das Brötchen (bread roll). A verb used as a noun is always das: das Essen, das Radfahren.

Reminder · Colour your notes. Write nouns in your notebook in colour: blue = der, red = die, green = das. Netzwerk neu uses the same colours.

3 · ein, eine (a / an) and kein, keine (no / not a)

der/die/das = a specific thing (the). ein/eine = any one thing (a). Das ist eine Lampe. Die Lampe ist schön. (That is a lamp. The lamp is beautiful.)

the (definite)a (indefinite)not a / no (negative)
maskulinder Tischein Tischkein Tisch
neutraldas Buchein Buchkein Buch
feminindie Lampeeine Lampekeine Lampe
Pluraldie Bücher— Bücher (nothing)keine Bücher
der + das = ein · die = eineOnly the feminine "die" adds an -e

Trick: "die" ends with e, so "eine" gets an e too. Same for kein → keine. der and das are twins: they share "ein".

Next step: Akkusativ (the object form)

When a noun is the thing you have, want or buy (after haben, möchten, brauchen, kaufen), only the der words change. You need this in Sprechen Teil 3.

Akkusativ: only der changesAfter haben, möchten, brauchen, kaufen

Trick: "der man needs an N": den, einen, keinen. Everybody else stays the same. Ich möchte einen Kaffee und eine Banane.

4 · Singular und Plural (one and many)

German has several plural endings. There is no single rule, so learn the plural with the noun: der Tisch, die Tische. Dictionaries write it short: Tisch, der, -e. The ¨ symbol means the vowel gets an umlaut: Buch, das, ¨-er → Bücher.

EndingTypical forSingular → Plural
-n / -enmost die wordsdie Lampe → die Lampen · die Wohnung → die Wohnungen
-nenfemale -indie Freundin → die Freundinnen
-e / ¨-emany der wordsder Tisch → die Tische · der Stuhl → die Stühle
-er / ¨-ermany das wordsdas Kind → die Kinder · das Buch → die Bücher
-sloan words, ending in a voweldas Handy → die Handys · das Auto → die Autos
– / ¨-er, -el, -chender Computer → die Computer · der Apfel → die Äpfel
Plural is always dieEvery noun, any gender: more than one = die

Trick: plural is a crowd, and the crowd always wears purple "die". So if you only know the plural, you can't guess the gender. Learn singular + plural together.

7 · Fragen bilden und antworten (building questions and answers)

Asking and answering is half of the speaking exam. Build every question with the same three steps, then answer by swapping the person.

W-Frage in 3 Schritten (W-question in 3 steps)

  1. W-word: what do you want to know? Place → Wo. Origin → Woher. Name → Wie. Time → Wann. Thing → Was.
  2. Verb: conjugate it for the person you ask: du wohnst, Sie wohnen.
  3. Person + rest: du / Sie, then the rest of the sentence. End with ?

Teil B

Buchstaben und Zahlen (letters and numbers)

The alphabet, numbers, the time and prices, with a pronunciation guide. Names, numbers and times are the biggest points in the exam's listening test.

8 · Das Alphabet (the alphabet)

In the exam you spell your name (Sprechen Teil 1), and in the listening test names are spelled to you. Under each letter: its German name, then an English sound guide. Letters with a red border are easy to confuse for English speakers.

Reminder · The tricky ones. E sounds like English "ay", I like English "ee". J = "yot", V = "fow", W = "vay", Z = "tset". When spelling double letters, say Doppel-L or just L, L. ß is called Eszett.

Trick: for ei and ie, say the English name of the second letter. e-i → "eye" (drei). i-e → "ee" (vier). ü: say "ee" with your lips round like "oo".

9 · Zahlen (numbers)

The pronunciation guide uses English sounds; CAPS = stressed syllable, kh = the soft "ch" like in "ich", ue = ü (say "ee" with rounded lips).

Numbers are backwards21–99: say the second digit first

Trick: like the old English rhyme "four-and-twenty blackbirds". When you hear a number, write the right digit first, then the left one. 47 = sieben·und·vierzig, 74 = vier·und·siebzig.

Reminder · ein and zwo. eins becomes ein inside a bigger number: einundzwanzig (AYN-oont-TSVAN-tsikh) = 21. On the phone people often say zwo (tsvoh) instead of zwei, so it doesn't sound like drei.
Reminder · Watch the spelling. sechzehn (16) and sechzig (60) drop the -s of sechs. siebzehn (17) and siebzig (70) drop the -en of sieben. dreißig (30) uses ß, not -zig.

Zahlen-Explorer (number explorer)

Reminder · Numbers in the exam. Phone numbers and postcodes are said digit by digit: 80331 = acht · null · drei · drei · eins. House numbers are one number: 47 = siebenundvierzig. Times: 19 Uhr = neunzehn Uhr (NOYN-tsayn oor).

Teil C

Themen (topics)

Ready-made sentences about you, your work, your free time and finding your way. Change them to fit your life.

10 · Über mich (about me)

The most important text you will ever say in German. In the exam (Sprechen Teil 1) you get a card with these seven keywords and say one sentence for each. Then the examiner asks you to spell your name and say a number. Fill in your details: the text updates with the English meaning. Practise it aloud until you can say it without reading.

Name? · Alter? · Land? · Wohnort? · Sprachen? · Beruf? · Hobby?
Reminder · No article before a job. Ich bin Ingenieurin. (not eine Ingenieurin). Women add -in: Ingenieur → Ingenieurin, Lehrer → Lehrerin.
Reminder · Countries. Most have no article: aus Brasilien, aus Deutschland, in Hamburg. A few do: aus der Schweiz, aus der Türkei, aus den USA.
Reminder · Languages and hobbies. Languages end in -isch and have a capital letter: Englisch, Portugiesisch, Chinesisch. A verb used as a hobby is a noun, so it gets a capital too: das Lesen, das Kochen. Or say it with gern: Ich koche gern. (I like cooking.)

14 · Goethe-Zertifikat A1 (the A1 exam)

The exam has four parts. You pass with 60 of 100 points (sechzig ZEKH-tsikh von hundert HOON-dert). Check the official Modellsatz (sample test) from the Goethe-Institut before the exam.

Sprechen · Teil 3

Bitten formulieren (make requests)

You get a picture card (for example a window or a pen). Ask your partner for something, and your partner reacts.

Bild (picture)Bitte (request)Reaktion (reaction)
Fenster (window)Kannst du bitte das Fenster öffnen? (Can you please open the window?)Ja, gern. (Yes, gladly.)
Kuli (pen)Hast du einen Kuli für mich? (Do you have a pen for me?)Ja, hier bitte. (Yes, here you are.)
KaffeeIch möchte bitte einen Kaffee. (I would like a coffee, please.)Gern. Mit Milch? (Sure. With milk?)
Wasser (water)Gib mir bitte das Wasser. (Give me the water, please.)Hier, bitte. (Here you are.)
Handy (phone)Darf ich dein Handy benutzen? (May I use your phone?)Nein, tut mir leid. (No, sorry.)
Reminder · Infinitive at the end. Kannst du bitte das Fenster öffnen? kann/möchte/darf comes early, the second verb goes to the end.
Reminder · Akkusativ: only der changes. After haben, möchten, brauchen: der → den, ein → einen. Ich möchte einen Kaffee. die, das and plural stay the same: eine Banane, ein Brot.

Jetzt üben (practise now)

Lerntipps (study tips)

Small habits that help the rules stick.

  • Colour sticky notes at home: blue for der, red for die, green for das. Stick them on things: der Tisch, die Lampe, das Bett.
  • Three rooms in your head: der nouns live in a blue room, die nouns in a red room, das nouns in a green room. When you learn a noun, picture it in its room.
  • Say the noun package aloud three times: der Tisch, die Tische. Article + noun + plural, always together.
  • Numbers everywhere: read prices in the supermarket, bus numbers and house numbers in German, in your head.
  • Spell your name and street once a day: M – A – R – I – A (emm – ah – err – ee – ah).
