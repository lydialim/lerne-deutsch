1der · die · das (how German assigns gender)

Why does German have three genders, and how can you guess them? First the story, then some surprising facts, then the patterns that help you.

1 · Die Geschichte (the story)

Woher kommen drei Genera? (where the three genders came from)

  1. Early Proto-Indo-European: two classesProbably animate and inanimate, not male and female. Hittite, the oldest recorded Indo-European language, kept only this two-way split.
  2. Later: the feminine appearsThe animate class split in two. A widely held theory says the new feminine grew out of a suffix that formed abstract and collective nouns. This may be why abstract nouns still lean feminine (die Freiheit).
  3. Old High German (about 750–1050)German inherited all three genders. Endings on the noun wore away, so the article took over the job of showing gender and case.
  4. English and Dutch: cousins that dropped a genderEnglish has only one word for “the”. Old English once had three genders like German, but lost them by the Middle English period. Dutch, a close cousin of German, merged masculine and feminine into one group. So it has two words for “the”: de (de man, de vrouw) and het (het kind). German kept all three: der, die, das.
  5. 1880: Mark Twain's jokeThe American writer Mark Twain learned German and wrote a funny essay about it, “The Awful German Language”. He joked that in German “a young lady has no sex, while a turnip has”. He meant: das Mädchen (girl) is neuter, but die Rübe (turnip) is feminine. The neuter Mädchen is purely grammatical: the ending -chen makes every word neuter. Read about the essay · full text (Appendix D, free)
  6. TodayNew loanwords get a gender within a few years. Regions still differ (das / die Cola), and some words accept two genders (das / der Virus).

2 · Überraschende Fakten (surprising facts)

Gut zu wissen (good to know)

The ending wins over the meaning.
das Mädchen and das Fräulein are neuter although they mean girls and women. die Wache (guard) is feminine even for a man.
Gender is not about sex.
“Car” is das Auto in German, la voiture in French and el coche in Spanish. It is a noun class, a filing system.
It helps listeners.
Hearing der or die rules out many nouns before the noun comes.
Children learn the patterns early.
Research on children learning German suggests they pick up ending and sound patterns early, long before they know any rules.

3 · Die Muster (the patterns)

Wie oft? (how common is each?)

Merke: die is the most common gender, so it is the best guess when you know nothing else.

Plural: always die
der Tisch → die Tische · das Kind → die Kinder · die Frau → die Frauen
Learn the gender only in the singular.

💡 Trick: about 80 % of nouns can be worked out from their form. That is far fewer to memorise than it feels like.

    Regional (both can be right)

    Zusammengesetzte und fremde Wörter (compounds and loanwords)

    Compound: the last part decides.
    die Donaudampfschifffahrtsgesellschaft is feminine because of -schaft.
    Only the last part counts, however long the word is.

    New and borrowed words get a gender by these strategies, roughly in this order (according to the Duden, as summarised by the University of Michigan):

    1. Keep the original gender: das Museum, der Alligator
    2. Copy a German equivalent: der Computer (der Rechner) · die Mail (die Post)
    3. Follow the ending: die Garage, die Pizza · das Management
    4. English one-syllable verbs → der: der Chat, der Drink, der Sound

    Und danach? Fälle (the article changes with the case)

    The gender stays fixed. The article changes with the job of the noun in the sentence. At A1 you need the nominative and the accusative.

    Dative and genitive come later (A2 and above). Only the masculine changes in the accusative: der → den.

    💡 Plural trick: -en plurals are mostly feminine nouns (die Frau → die Frauen). -er plurals are never feminine (das Kind → die Kinder).

    So lernst du es (how to learn it)

    • Patterns first. The rule endings in step 2 cover thousands of words.
    • Colour your cards. blue = der · red = die · green = das · purple = plural.
    • Article + plural. Learn der Tisch, die Tische, never just “Tisch”.
